2. Data collection

data collection is monstrously difficult, and very domain specific.

julia’s eye ball anecdote. Are you going to throw that information away because it’s not perfect, or are you going to make do? Stats and data science: “learning to make do with the data you actually have.” Wring all of the juice out of it and acknolwedge the limitations of your results in the most faithful and transparent and informative way.

what’s the ideal experiment a la angrist and pischke.

Some controlled thing. Talk about study design and what’s special about randomized controlled experiments.

Can we do this? maybe?

site seattle minimum wage study. But it’s costly (time, money, you name it).

what about observational data? can’t control anything

data collection was simply a survey. What do you do if people don’t respond? Why didn’t they respond? What if their responses are not accurate or if they’re guessing?

You try to do the best you can with the info you have, but it’s far from perfect, so how are you going to handle that uncertainty? that’s what we’re all about ti statistics.

the anatomy of a data frame: thw rows and the columns

4. Modeling

Data do not speak for themselves. You need some sort of interpretive lens. The most rigorous form of lens is a mathematical model.

modeling produces a result. How reliable is this result? If we kept surveying restaurants, would this result remain stable, or would it change? How sensitive is the result to our particular data?

sampling variability.
